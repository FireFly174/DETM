# Energy pattern taxonomy in DETM

Status: canonical document.
Scope: mechanisms / energy-pattern classes.

Note: first-pass translation of `docs/rus/20_mechanisms/energy_patterns_taxonomy.md`.

This card fixes a baseline taxonomy of energy patterns observed in DETM.
Classification is based on localization mode, transfer regime, and boundary role, not visual geometry.

The taxonomy is extended only by experimentally confirmed regimes.

---

## 0. Classification principle

An energy pattern in DETM is:

> a stable mode of energy motion under representability constraints.

Axes:
1. localization mode (volume / boundary / point);
2. energy transfer type;
3. temporal stability;
4. scaling behavior;
5. entropy and internal-time role.

---

## 1. Background patterns

### 1.1 Uniform background

- weak gradients;
- minimal dynamics;
- no stable boundaries.

Control state. Does not form invariants.

### 1.2 Noisy background

- small-scale fluctuations;
- high local entropy;
- short correlation time;
- no stable coarsening.

Source of fluctuations and precursor material for other patterns.

---

## 2. Localized static patterns

### 2.1 Energy domains

Local regions with near-constant energy.
- suppressed internal motion;
- quasi-stationary interior;
- boundary carries most dynamics.

### 2.2 Binary domains (0/1 phases)

Sharp split between near-0 and near-1 regions.
- frozen volume;
- thin active boundary layer;
- minimal interior dynamics.

Limit regime near level coarsening.

---

## 3. Boundary patterns

### 3.1 Static boundaries

Stable interfaces between domains.
- localized dynamics;
- stable shape;
- spectral identity.

### 3.2 Moving fronts

Boundaries propagating across space.
- stable propagation speed;
- shape preservation;
- transport of regime (not simple mass packet transport).

---

## 4. Oscillatory patterns

### 4.1 Local oscillators

Bounded regions with periodic dynamics.
- own internal time;
- spectral stability;
- localization.

### 4.2 Boundary oscillators

Oscillation localized at boundaries.
- interior effectively frozen;
- dynamics concentrated at interface;
- often highly stable.

---

## 5. Flow patterns

### 5.1 Linear flows

Sustained directed transfer.
- depends on internal-time regime;
- entropy-sensitive;
- limited scalability.

### 5.2 Circulation flows

Closed transfer loops.
- bounded circulation area;
- robust to local perturbations;
- linked to vortex-like modes.

---

## 6. Crystalline patterns

### 6.1 Crystal of local invariants

Regular structure built from repeated minimal stable patterns.
- no single global invariant;
- repeated local motif;
- often a non-scalability signature.

---

## 7. Critical and transitional patterns

### 7.1 Critical sieves

Alternation of active and frozen zones.
- high sensitivity;
- boundary fractalization tendency;
- temporal instability.

### 7.2 Pattern collapse

Loss of distinguishability and transition to a coarser representational level.
Interpretation: change of representation form, not annihilation.

---

## 8. Meta-class: invariants

Any pattern that:
- preserves form over time;
- has a stable spectrum;
- survives moderate perturbations;

is treated as a DETM invariant.

Invariants belong to dynamics, not to static geometry.

---

## 9. Canonical fixation

DETM pattern classes are defined by localization/transfer regime,
not by geometry or absolute scale.
